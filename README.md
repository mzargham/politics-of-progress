# The Politics of Progress

**_The Politics of Progress_** is a long-form comparative and meta-analytical essay that examines two influential texts—Marc Andreessen’s *Techno-Optimist Manifesto* and Ezra Klein & Derek Thompson’s *Abundance*—to map the contemporary Overton window on technology, political economy, and governance.

There are three parts:
1. **Comparative Analysis** of the authors’ stated positions on shared themes such as technological progress, institutional design, markets, regulation, and visions of the future.
2. **Meta Analysis** of the epistemic and ideological assumptions underlying both texts, revealing a shared neo-modernist framing.
3. **Synthesis and Takeaways** that propose a metamodern alternative: treating governance as adaptive constraint negotiation, grounded in polycentricity, subsidiarity, and systemic resilience.

The essay draws on insights from systems theory, political philosophy, and ecological design to argue that the future of governance must move beyond throughput optimization toward institutional ecosystems capable of coordinating plural values under shared constraints.
